# Chapter 8 — Interdictum

The Galleon grows warm at ten past five in the morning.

Josh wasn't asleep. He hadn't been for hours. At midnight, Atis had appeared outside the entrance to the Slytherin common room, in the damp dungeon corridor, with her hood up and dried blood on her lip. She wasn't crying. She was shaking. She told him, very quietly, that she had woken up in the shelter he had built in the forest, with no memory of how she'd got there. That someone was calling her from the trees with her brother's voice. That then, over that voice, she had heard another.

"Yours," she told him, touching the collar that hung around her neck. "You told me to come back. And I came back."

He hadn't said anything to her. He'd been asleep in the dungeons, half a castle away.

He wiped her lip with his sleeve. He promised her that on Saturday night they would go to the Manor to look for answers. She nodded, gave him a short kiss on the jaw and went back to Hufflepuff.

Now the Galleon burns against his ribs. On the rim, the numbers rearrange themselves into a single word.

*MANOR.*

---

Greengrass Manor at six in the morning is grey inside and out, except for the reddish flashes of Mrs. Greengrass's red roses, which Elf never stops tending even though she's no longer there.

Josh arrives by way of the tapestry passage, the Hogsmeade hedge, Apparition. He crosses the gate in the usual drizzle and enters by the front door, which the elf opens before he touches it. There's no one in the entrance hall. In the drawing room, there is.

Corvus stands beside the cold fireplace, taking off his gloves. He isn't alone.

To his left, a boy Josh's age, perhaps a year older, with dark, tousled hair, a smile that doesn't reach his eyes and his hands in the pockets of an expensive cloak. Mattheo Riddle. Josh knows him from the meetings, from the long tables, from the corners where the things not said at the table get said. He's never liked the way he looks at people's throats.

To his right, a young woman with two blonde streaks in her brown hair, pinned up with almost surgical precision, in a grey fur coat. Olivia Malfoy. She has her family's face: pale, fine, bored in advance.

And in the middle of the drawing room, tied to a dining chair someone brought from the next room, there's a man.

Forty, perhaps. Several days' stubble. One eye swollen. They've taken his wand, his shoes and his belt. He breathes through his mouth. When Josh comes in, he raises his head, looks at him, and something like hope forms on his face, lasting as long as it takes him to see the green and grey scarf.

"Greengrass," says Corvus. "Forgive the hour. The legacy doesn't always choose when."

"Corvus."

"You know Mattheo. Miss Malfoy."

"Greengrass," says Mattheo, with half a smile. "Nice house. A bit cold."

Olivia says nothing. She looks at Josh's father's armchair, still facing the room and not the window, the way one appraises a piece of furniture at auction.

"Who is he?" asks Josh.

"No one," says Corvus. "That's the problem. He'd been with us three years, in a cell in the south. And for the last eight months he's been selling names to the Ministry." He sits on the arm of the armchair, unhurried. "Minor names. Isolated incidents. Things that can be filed."

Josh doesn't move. He doesn't look at the man. He doesn't look at Corvus. He looks at a point on the wall between them, where the green paper has a damp stain no one has ever fixed.

"What we want to know," Corvus goes on, "is who he was selling them to. The name of his contact. And he, unfortunately, doesn't want to tell us."

"You could ask him another way."

"We tried." Mattheo shrugs. "He's tough. You have to give him that."

"Which is why we're here," says Corvus. "Your spell, Greengrass. The one you're stuck on. The one that will move you up a chair. A mind shut, locked within itself, that cannot lie to whoever opens it." A pause. "Carcer Mentis."

The name falls into the drawing room like something breaking.

Josh knows it better than anyone in that room, which is why he never says it out loud. Carcer Mentis doesn't read a mind: it imprisons it. His blood tangles with the other person's blood and, through it, with everything else: the nerves, the memories, the place where a person hides from themselves. And it leaves them trapped in there, inside their own subconscious, in an agony that doesn't end for as long as the ritual lasts. A locked mind has nowhere to run. Whoever holds the threads can walk through it and take whatever they want, the truth included. Josh learned it only once, at fifteen, in front of something that had no name. He hasn't mastered it. It's the only ritual that, when it ends, erases whole minutes from his memory: he never remembers doing it, only what's left afterward.

"I haven't mastered it," says Josh.

"I know."

"If I haven't mastered it, it can go wrong."

"I know that too." Corvus stands. Comes closer. He speaks low, only to him, though the other two can hear. "You've been studying it for months. You've read everything there is to read. All you're missing is doing it. What happened three years ago won't happen again. Whatever happens to this one won't matter. He'll be dead by morning anyway."

Josh looks at the man in the chair for the first time.

The man looks back at him. He has pale eyes, bloodshot. He doesn't plead. He's already understood there's no one to plead with.

*He's been selling names to the Ministry.* Minor names. Isolated incidents. Things that can be filed.

Josh unbuttons his left cuff.

---

He ties the cloth around his wrist. Two turns.

He takes out the dagger.

The cut is long this time, from the wrist toward the elbow, and the blood comes out red and darkens in the air, and doesn't fall. It stays trembling on the skin, waiting. Josh says the words, in the language that scrapes, slowly, each one whole.

The blood stretches toward the man in the chair. A fine thread. Two. Three. They circle his head without touching him, like the bars of a cage that hasn't finished closing.

Then the cold comes.

Not in his eyes. In his arm. It climbs from inside, from the cut, like ice water entering through the wound and creeping up the veins, and the arm goes rigid, hard as wood, and the black lines climb past the elbow, fast, too fast, toward the shoulder, toward the neck.

Something speaks.

Not with a voice. With the voice of his own blood. It says his name. It says another name, which he can't repeat.

The man in the chair screams. Or doesn't. Josh looks at him, and the man's face

the face is another face. One second. A familiar face over the stranger's face, like two drawings on the same sheet of tracing paper. Dark hair. Calculating eyes. A mouth that knows how to say *seven, I think, I never knew whether there were eight*

it's his face.

It's his own face, tied to the chair.

No.

---

Then, the floor.

Josh is on his knees on the drawing-room carpet. He doesn't remember kneeling. He remembers nothing between the face in the chair and the carpet. There's a smooth space where the minutes should be.

There's something hot on his cheeks. He touches it. His fingers come away red. Blood. It runs down his face from his eyes, like tears that aren't tears.

His left arm burns as if it had been thrust into fire.

He raises his head.

The man in the chair is still. His eyes are open, fixed on a point on the ceiling. Blood runs from his nose, his ears, the corners of his eyes. His chest doesn't move. The ropes are still tied, but they no longer hold anyone: they hold something that weighs like a sack.

"Well," says Mattheo, from somewhere. "That was one way to open him."

"Shut up," says Olivia, without raising her voice. She's putting on her gloves. There's a drop of blood on the edge of her fur coat, and she looks at it with irritation.

Corvus is crouched in front of Josh. Josh didn't see him move. He's looking at his hands, his arm, the black lines that still won't go down.

"How long?" asks Josh. His voice comes out hoarse, as if he'd been screaming.

"Four minutes," says Corvus. "Maybe five."

"Did he say anything?"

"He screamed. A lot." Corvus stands. "And then, nothing."

Josh looks at the body. It isn't the first time he's seen one. It isn't the first time he's seen one that was his fault. But always, until today, there was a calculation first. A line that said *necessary*, a sum that balanced. He looks for the calculation. There isn't one. There's a man tied to a chair, without a wand, without shoes, who couldn't defend himself against anything.

And there are five minutes that aren't there.

"You still haven't mastered it," says Corvus. It isn't a reproach. It's a fact.

"I know."

"Then learn." He pulls on a glove, finger by finger. "Faster. More often." He pulls on the other. "You have more to look after now, Greengrass. It would suit you to take command."

He doesn't say her name. He doesn't need to. The rest of the sentence says all of it.

Mattheo and Olivia untie the body. One of them puts a hand on its shoulder and the three of them vanish through the fireplace with it, in a green flare, without saying goodbye. The chair remains, the cut ropes hanging from its rails. A dark stain remains on the carpet.

Josh stays on his knees for a long while.

"Elf," he says at last.

The creature appears, ears flat against its head.

"The carpet. The chair. Leave nothing."

"Yes, master."

"And don't call me that."

The elf vanishes. Josh wipes his face with his sleeve. The sleeve comes away red. He looks at his hands.

It takes him a second too long to be sure they're his.

---

At eleven at night he comes back with Atis.

She arrives at the gate with her hood up and the collar inside her coat. On the gravel drive she stops to look at the whole house, the dark windows, the bare oaks, the drizzle that can't make up its mind.

"It's enormous," she says. "And sad. Sorry. It's enormous and sad."

"Both things are true."

In the entrance hall, the elf lights the lamps without being asked. Atis looks at the portraits in the gallery with her mouth slightly open, and the one with the white ruff stares back at her with contempt and mutters something about visitors at indecent hours. She apologizes to him too, and the portrait is so taken aback that it doesn't answer.

They pass the drawing-room door. It's closed. It smells, faintly, of elf soap and of something else beneath it that the soap doesn't quite cover.

"What's that smell?" she asks.

"They cleaned today."

She nods. She doesn't ask what they cleaned.

---

The traditional library takes up the whole west wing of the first floor. Shelves to the ceiling, ladders on wheels, long oak tables with green-shaded lamps. Josh lights three with a flick of his wand, and Atis turns in a circle in the middle of the room, looking at the spines with a hunger for knowledge he didn't know she had.

"You grew up here," she says. "With all this."

"I grew up near all this."

They start with the general library: three volumes. A treatise on the magical forests of Northern Europe. A history of the wizarding schools of the continent, with a whole chapter on Durmstrang. A compendium of territorial curses: places that choose whom to hurt.

Atis reads fast, a finger under the line, murmuring under her breath. Josh reads slowly. Now and then he looks up and watches her, the way she bites her lip when something doesn't add up, the collar showing between the folds of her coat, the trace of dried blood still at the base of her nose.

"Here," she says, after an hour. "Listen. *'There are forests in the north that keep the voices of those who went in and did not come out. Not the real voice: the hollow it left. And they offer that hollow to those who miss them.'*" She looks up. "That's it. That's exactly it. I hear people who aren't there."

Josh thinks of a man with several days' stubble and his eyes fixed on the ceiling. He pushes the thought away.

"Does it say how to stop it?"

"No. It says you have to find 'the seal of the place.' And nothing else." She turns the page, turns it back. "I hate it when books do that."

Josh gets up to fetch the fourth book: a study of Scandinavian protective runes that he knows is in the library, that he read last summer, that has pencil underlining.

He can't find it.

He goes over the shelf where it should be. Then the next one. Then the Celtic runes, in case he put it back wrong. Nothing. He stands in front of the wall of books with his wand lit, and feels something that has no name yet, something like standing in a familiar room with the lights off.

"Elf."

The creature appears beside him.

"The book of Scandinavian runes. The brown one. Where is it?"

The elf looks at him. Blinks, with those enormous eyes, too round.

"On the side table, sir," it says softly. "Next to the reading chair. Where sir always leaves it."

Josh looks toward the corner. The reading chair, his, with the blanket folded over the arm. The side table beside it. And on the side table, the brown book, a ribbon marker sticking out between the pages.

Where he always leaves it.

"Thank you," he says.

The elf vanishes. Atis hasn't looked up from the compendium. Josh goes to the side table, picks up the book and returns to the table, and sits, and opens it at the marker, and doesn't remember marking it.

---

By one in the morning there's nothing useful left in the traditional library.

"There's another one," says Josh.

Atis raises her head.

"The grimoire library. The blood one."

"Is it here?"

"Upstairs. At the end of the corridor."

She closes the book slowly.

"Can I go in?"

"You can go in if I open it."

The door is at the end of the second-floor corridor, where the gallery ends and the portraits stop hanging. It's black oak, with no handle, no lock. In the center, carved into the wood, there's a rune: three spirals joined at a single center, like three arms holding one another up. A Celtic rune of protection, older than the house.

"It's beautiful," Atis whispers.

"It asks for the blood of the line."

He unbuttons his left cuff. He does it slowly, because the arm still hurts from the morning, and when the sleeve rises, the black lines that never quite went away are in view. Atis looks at them. She says nothing yet.

He cuts his palm. He lays his hand on the rune.

The wood warms beneath his skin. The spirals fill with red, slowly, like channels flooding. And then the door pulls. It pulls at him, at his blood, inward, and Josh feels the cold climb his left arm and then, impossibly, his right as well, though the right touches nothing. The veins darken in both arms at once, from the wrists to the shoulders, black, marked, like roots beneath the skin.

The door opens with a soft click.

Josh takes his hand away. He waits for the lines to clear, as they always do.

They don't clear.

They fade a little. They go grey instead of black. But they stay: fine lines, like bruises drawn with a quill, climbing both forearms and disappearing beneath his sleeves.

Atis takes a step toward him. She takes his wrist, carefully, as she did in the dormitory the night of the dagger. She turns his arm toward the light.

"Josh." She says it very quietly. "What are these marks?"

---

He was ten and Tori had been dead seven days.

Corvus had come the afternoon before. Through the fireplace again, in the too-new cloak again. He had sat in his father's armchair and talked for a long time in a soft voice. He had told him that the ones who killed her were still alive. That they were on the other side, with their Auror cloaks and their reports and their inventories, sleeping soundly. That Greengrass blood could reach places no wand could. That no one else in the house could open the books.

"She couldn't," he had told him. "You can. She would have wanted it."

He didn't know whether she would have wanted it. She had never let him go up to the second floor at night.

That night he went up.

The door at the end of the corridor was taller than he was. So was the rune. In his hand he held Tori's brooch, the silver one, the one she used to fasten her grey coat; he had taken it from the drawer that same afternoon, without anyone seeing. He pricked his finger with the pin. A drop came out. He pressed it to the center of the spirals.

The wood drank.

And pulled.

It was as if someone had grabbed his arm from the inside and dragged it. The cold climbed to his shoulder. He looked at his arm in the candlelight and saw the veins, black, thick, standing out from his skin like something that had always been there and was only now letting itself be seen.

The door opened.

Inside it smelled of dust and iron. There were shelves of books with no titles on their spines. A table. And on the floor, in the center, a circle. Not chalk. Something dark, dry, ancient, that had soaked into the stone and never come out. There, he had been told once, an ancestor had knelt centuries ago and spoken with something that had no name. There it had all begun. The dagger. The price. The blood that knows how to do things.

He didn't go past the threshold.

He stood looking at the circle. Then he looked at his arm. The black veins. The white skin around them. It didn't look like his arm. It looked like someone else's. Someone who had just opened that door.

He ran down the stairs. Got into bed. Pulled the covers over his head.

In the morning, the marks were gone. Almost. A shadow remained in the crook of his elbow, which took three days to go.

He didn't go up again for more than a year.

But he learned, that night, that his arm could belong to someone else.

And he learned to button his sleeves.

---

"Josh?"

Atis is still holding his wrist. Seconds have passed. Many. He sees it in her face.

"They're from the door," he says. His voice comes out even. "The seal charges."

"But they're not going away."

"They used to."

She runs her thumb over one of the grey lines, slowly, as if she could erase it.

"Do they hurt?"

"A little." It's true. It's a small part of the truth. "More cold than pain."

"Like putting your arm in a river in winter," she says. She remembers. She remembers everything.

"Like that."

She doesn't let go of his wrist until they're inside.

---

The grimoire library is small. Smaller than she expected, it shows. Shelves of untitled books. A table. The circle on the floor, and salt. Salt again, which she looks at for a second and then decides not to look at. Josh lights a single candle.

They search two books. The only ones he knows speak of pacts with places.

The first is a treatise on territorial blood rituals, handwritten by someone who signed only with an initial. It speaks of carved stones that lock away whatever hunger a place holds. Four stones, always four, arranged around a ritual disc, with runes that are painted red once a generation.

"I've seen them," Atis says suddenly. She leans over the page. "At Durmstrang. Past the campsite, in some ruins. Four stones with red runes. And a disc on the ground. I thought it was decorative."

"It isn't decorative."

"What do they lock away?"

Josh turns the page.

*"The stones seal the blood moon. While the seal holds, what the forest keeps remains asleep. When the seal cracks…"*

The sentence continues on the next page.

The next page isn't there.

It's been torn out. Long ago: the edge is yellowed, frayed, and someone has stuck a paper strip over the seam to keep the book from falling apart. Josh runs his finger along the gap.

"Who tore it out?" asks Atis.

"I don't know."

The second book is worse. It mentions an observatory, "built by monks who studied the sky to predict the moons that must not be looked upon," in a forest in northern Europe. It doesn't say which. It doesn't say what happened to the monks. The last ten pages are written in a cipher Josh doesn't recognize, letters that look like runes but aren't runes, in an ink that by candlelight is the color of rust.

"Can you read it?" she asks.

"No."

"Can anyone?"

"I don't know."

Atis looks at the ciphered pages for a long time. Then she brings her hand to the collar, over her coat, without noticing.

"We're going to keep looking," she says. It isn't a question.

"We're going to keep looking."

---

They close the door at three in the morning. The rune goes back to being wood.

In the corridor, beneath the sleeping portraits, Atis stops. She takes the collar out from under her coat and holds it in her palm. A fine chain, dark. A small pendant, with a drop of something red sealed inside that isn't stone.

"Last night," she says, "when you spoke to me. From the collar." She looks at it. "You were asleep, weren't you? You weren't really talking to me."

"No."

"But it was your voice."

"It's my blood. It speaks with what it has."

She closes her hand around the pendant.

"It brought me back anyway."

He doesn't answer.

The same blood. The blood sealed in that drop, against her chest, beating slowly against the cloth. The blood that this morning stretched in three threads around the head of a man tied to a chair, in the drawing room below, where it still smells of soap.

The same blood that brought her back from the forest is the blood that couldn't bring anyone back.

Atis tucks the collar under her coat. She takes his hand. Without noticing, she runs her thumb over one of the grey lines on his wrist.

Josh doesn't pull down his sleeve.

For the first time in eight years, he walks with the marks in view and lets someone look at them.
