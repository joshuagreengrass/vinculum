# Chapter 7 — Nuntius

In the Highlands the mist doesn't fall: it walks down.

Josh watches it come along the slope as they climb the last stretch of the path, a slow white mass that swallows first the pines up top, then the rocks, then the stream. Atis walks beside him with her hood up and her hands tucked into the sleeves of her coat, looking at everything with wide eyes, as if every rock were a creature she hasn't been introduced to yet.

"Are you sure it's this way?"

"Sure."

"There's no sign."

"That's why nobody comes."

They left the castle through the tapestry passage mid-morning, crossed Hogsmeade by the back streets, and at the usual hedge he offered her his arm and took her by Side-Along Apparition. She landed dizzy, laughing, clutching his sleeve with both hands. Now the village, the school and the rest of the world lie at a distance that isn't measured in miles.

The hut appears suddenly out of the mist: grey stone, slate roof, smoke rising from the chimney. Behind it, greenhouses of fogged glass and a wooden fence that disappears up the slope. A small hand-carved sign, hanging from a nail: *Nature Reserve for Magical Creatures. Kindly do not feed anything.*

Atis reads it aloud and laughs.

"It's a reserve," she says, like someone who has just got a joke. "You brought me to a reserve."

"It isn't an excuse to bring you somewhere pretty."

"No?"

"No." He stops in front of the door. "We came to do something. I haven't told you what yet, because I wanted you to see it."

She looks at him. Stops smiling, not entirely.

"Okay," she says. "Now I'm nervous."

---

The magizoologist is a big man, grey-bearded, his hands covered in the scars of old bites, who opens the door before they knock. He greets Josh with a long handshake and Atis with a curious nod.

"You came," he says to Josh. "I wasn't sure."

"I told you I'd come."

"You told me, once, that you could do this." The man scratches his beard. "Years ago. You were a skinny boy with an egg in a box."

Atis turns her head toward Josh. He doesn't look at her.

"Cissa," he says, by way of explanation. "The egg wasn't supposed to survive. They pulled it through."

"Cissa is a…?"

"A dragon."

"You have a dragon?"

"I owe these people something," says Josh, who doesn't answer the question. "Something that isn't paid in Galleons."

The magizoologist leads them to the last greenhouse. Inside it's warm and smells of damp earth and sap. In the middle, clinging to a moss-covered trunk, is an animal the size of a large cat, green and mottled, with long frog's legs and fingers that grip the bark. There's a deep gash in its side. The edges are grey. It isn't bleeding; it has almost nothing left to bleed.

"A Clabbert," Atis whispers, and her voice shows she recognizes it the way one recognizes someone in an old photograph. "They live in trees. The pustule on the forehead lights up when they sense danger. Like an alarm. My dad used to say some Muggles kept them in their gardens instead of guard dogs, centuries ago."

"Ointments, potions, everything," says the magizoologist. "It won't close. Whatever did that had something on its claws. Whatever it is, it won't let the flesh remember."

Josh takes a step toward the trunk.

The Clabbert's pustule lights up.

Red. A vivid, burning red that pulses on the animal's forehead like an eye. Alarm. Danger. Josh stops. Blinks once too many.

"It knows what I am," he says quietly.

---

He explains while he prepares, because she asks and because he wants her to ask.

"I'm going to bind it to me. My blood will hold up its own while the wound closes. I've done it before with a plant." He takes a dark cloth from his robes and wraps it around his left wrist, two turns, tight. "This one breathes. It's different."

"Why bind it?" Atis has her hands clasped against her chest. "I mean, what does it actually do?"

"The wound doesn't need medicine. It needs something to hold it while the tissue remembers how to heal. A blood bond lends continuity. I'm not using the animal. I'm holding it up." He pushes up his sleeve. "And the pact ends with it when it heals."

"Because you don't break a sealed pact."

He looks up. She remembers. She remembers every single thing he told her in the dark, beneath the canopy.

"Because I don't break a sealed pact."

"You can wait outside," he adds, more quietly. "It isn't pretty to watch."

She thinks about it. Looks at the Clabbert, the grey gash, the lit pustule. Looks at him.

"I'm staying."

---

The cut is short, on the forearm, with the dagger.

The blood comes out red and outside it darkens, almost black, dense. It doesn't fall. It rises. It lifts from the cut in a fine, taut thread that crosses the humid air of the greenhouse to the Clabbert's side and sinks into the wound without spattering. The veins of his forearm darken from wrist toward elbow, like ink running beneath paper.

Josh says the words.

They aren't Latin. They aren't any language taught in any class. They're harsh, old, with consonants that scrape the throat, and he says them slowly, each one whole, out loud, the way a mistake he doesn't fully remember taught him to.

The Clabbert stops trembling.

The pustule is still red. Pulsing. Josh looks at it, and

He's back.

The pustule fades from red to ochre. From ochre to a pale green, dull, calm. The grey edges of the gash turn red. Slowly, the flesh begins to draw together on its own, like two banks seeking each other.

Beside him, Atis doesn't move. She has one hand pressed against her side, as if she had to hold it there so as not to reach toward the animal. She watches it all with her mouth slightly open. It's the first time she's seen him do something like this. Josh knows without looking at her.

---

Afterward, he can't feel his fingers.

He sits on a wooden bench against the greenhouse wall, the cloth still on his wrist. He's pale. Beneath the skin of his forearm, the veins have a greenish tint that's slow to fade. The magizoologist brings him a cup of something hot that smells of ginger, and he holds it in both hands without drinking.

"It'll sleep a whole day," he says, looking at the Clabbert, which has already closed its eyes against the trunk.

"Uh-huh," says Atis, sitting down beside him.

She asks him questions. Many. He answers.

The veins: it depends on the ritual. With the big ones it's worse. Hence the sleeves. It hardly hurts, actually. It's more cold than pain, like putting your arm in a river in winter. The real pain isn't in the moment. It's the kind that builds up.

The words: always the same for each ritual. It's the language of the demon the family made the deal with. He doesn't say its name. He doesn't write it. He doesn't pronounce it. That's a condition of the pact.

"And if you bind something wrong?" she asks. "What happens?"

Josh looks at the cup.

"It happened to me once. Years ago. Not with an animal." A pause. "It cost the one who did the binding more than the one who was bound."

She waits for more. He doesn't give her more.

"Not today," he says.

"Not today," she repeats. And doesn't push.

Josh looks at the sleeping animal again.

"It'll sleep a whole day."

Atis glances at him sideways. She says nothing. He doesn't notice he's already said it.

She gets up and approaches the Clabbert carefully, without touching the wound, like someone who learned how as a girl, kneeling in the grass beside a very old man who explained where to put her fingers. She feels for the pulse in its neck. She frowns.

"It should be worse," she says. "With a wound like that. And the pulse… it's very slow. Very even. A Clabbert doesn't beat like that."

"It's my blood," he says. "Holding up its own. The bond runs both ways. I feel it now. What it feels. It's like a door someone is pushing from the other side."

She draws her fingers back from the animal's neck, slowly, as if she were suddenly touching something of his.

"Keep looking," Josh asks her. "I want to know what else you see."

She looks again. She describes the skin, the mottling, the way the fingers loosen on the bark. He listens with his eyes half closed. At some point she says the Bowtruckle is breathing better, and he nods, and repeats:

"The Bowtruckle's breathing better."

She turns around with one eyebrow raised.

"Clabbert, Greengrass. Clabbert. Bowtruckles are twigs with arms. If you mix up a Clabbert and a Bowtruckle in the N.E.W.T., I'll fail you myself."

"Clabbert."

"Thank you."

He smiles, because she smiles. He isn't sure at what point she said "Bowtruckle." He isn't sure she said it.

---

They leave as the mist begins to lift.

At the door of the hut, the magizoologist is talking with someone. A tall man in a dark grey travelling cloak, well cut, and leather gloves. His back is to the path. He holds a folder with the reserve's crest embroidered on the cover and nods at something the magizoologist is explaining.

Josh recognizes him by the way he takes off his gloves.

Finger by finger.

"Ah," says Corvus Lestrange, turning as if he had only just noticed there was someone else on the hillside. "Greengrass. What a surprise."

It isn't a surprise. Josh knows that, or thinks he knows it. Or doesn't know it and that's why he thinks it.

"Corvus."

"Mr. Lestrange is on the board of trustees," says the magizoologist, with the awkward deference of someone who depends on another's money. "He comes once a month to go over the accounts."

"Once a month," Corvus confirms. "Today happened to be the day." He smiles, and the smile shifts, courteous, impeccable, toward Atis. "And you are…"

"This is Amatis," says Josh. "A classmate from Hogwarts. Atis, this is Mr. Lestrange. A friend of the family."

"Nice to meet you," says Atis, and holds out her hand.

Corvus shakes it. With his bare hand, no glove. He holds it just long enough.

"The pleasure is mine." And then, the way one asks the time: "Amatis what?"

"Castairs Scamander."

"Scamander." Corvus repeats it slowly, like someone tasting a wine whose vintage he already knew. "Of course. Your father wrote the book we all had at Hogwarts. *Fantastic Beasts.* I remember it fondly." He lets go of her hand. "It's natural that a magizoologist's daughter should end up in a place like this. Families have their inclinations."

"I suppose so," says Atis, smiling, understanding nothing.

Josh understands everything.

"Might I have a moment with Joshua, Miss Scamander?" says Corvus. "Family matters. Terribly dull. I promise to return him in one piece."

Atis looks at Josh. He nods.

"I'll wait for you on the path," she says, and heads off down the slope, hood up, turning back only once.

---

Corvus waits until she's far away. He doesn't speak until the mist has half swallowed her.

"A Clabbert," he says. "The magizoologist is very grateful. He told me in great detail. A blood bond, in broad daylight, in front of a witness who isn't of the house." He pulls on a glove. "Very generous of you."

"I owed the reserve something."

"I know. The reserve always keeps me informed about its contributors. That's the advantage of paying the bills." He pulls on the other glove. "How much did it cost you?"

"The usual."

"The usual is more each time." Corvus looks at his hands. Josh has them tucked into his sleeves, stretched down over his fingers, so the tint of the veins and the trembling won't show. "They're shaking."

"It's the cold."

"You feel the cold," says Corvus, and Josh feels something icy at the nape of his neck, because it's the same phrase he uses with her, the same excuse, and he doesn't know how Corvus knows it, or whether he knows it, or whether he chose it by chance.

Corvus looks toward the path. Toward the place where Atis's hood can barely be seen anymore.

"Scamander," he says again. "Half-blood. American mother, if I'm not mistaken. Outside the twenty-eight for two generations. At least."

Josh doesn't answer.

"I'm not going to ask you to leave her," says Corvus. "I'm not a man who asks for useless things."

"Then what."

Corvus adjusts his cloak. He takes his time.

"Remember what happened to the last person you couldn't afford to lose."

He says it and leaves. He doesn't say goodbye to the magizoologist. He doesn't look toward the path. He walks to the edge of the fence, where the mist is still thick, and turns, and is gone.

Josh stays standing at the door of the hut.

His hands are shaking inside his sleeves. This time it isn't the cold.

---

He finds her on the path, sitting on a rock, hugging her knees.

"Everything okay?" she asks, getting up.

"Everything's fine. Family matters."

"He seemed nice. Kind of intense." She brushes the dirt off her coat. "Was he a friend of your dad's?"

"Something like that."

She looks at him. He doesn't know what face he's wearing. He supposes the usual one. He feels it isn't the usual one.

Atis looks down at his hands. At the stretched sleeves that cover his fingers almost to the tips, wrinkled, uneven, like a boy wearing someone else's clothes.

"Look at the state of these," she says, in a tone of tender reproach.

And she takes his cuff with two fingers. A little tug. She folds the cloth back, one turn, slowly, until his hand shows. Then the other. She smooths the sleeve over his wrist with her palm, as if it were important work.

"There," she says. "Now I can see your hands."

---

It was the second night and he was still on the staircase.

The elves had brought him a blanket. He hadn't used it. The blanket was folded on the step beside him, and he was sitting halfway down the stairs, his shirt buttoned to the cuff, the button Astoria had fastened still fastened, because if he unfastened it, something. He didn't know what. Something.

The house was dark. The clock in the entrance hall was four minutes slow.

The fireplace flared green.

He stood up. So fast he felt dizzy. He went down two steps.

It wasn't her.

It was a young man. Very young. Taller than Tori, thinner, in a black cloak that was too new and hair that was neat, as if he'd combed it to come. He brushed the ash from his shoulders. He looked around the entrance hall as if he were learning it. Then he saw him on the staircase.

He went still.

"You must be Joshua," he said.

He didn't answer.

The young man took off his gloves. Finger by finger. Slowly, as if he needed to keep his hands busy with something.

"I was sent to speak with your family," he said. "But there's no one else, is there?"

He shook his head.

The young man climbed the first steps. He stopped two below him, so that their eyes were level. He didn't crouch. He didn't touch him. He spoke with a very correct courtesy, very rehearsed, like someone repeating a text he'd been given in writing.

"The house of Greengrass has made a sacrifice the legacy will not forget. Your sister was brave to the end." He paused, as if looking for the next line on a paper he didn't have in his hand. "There's no… Nothing could be brought back. I'm sorry."

He listened. He heard every word. None of them meant anything. All of them meant the same thing.

"She told me not to wait up for her," he said.

The young man looked at him for a long while. Something crossed his face, very quickly, something he hadn't yet learned to put away.

"Then you don't need to wait for her anymore," he said.

He looked down. At the shirt. At the buttoned cuff. He looked at it a second too long, as if he understood, or as if he were writing it down somewhere.

He said nothing about it.

He put his gloves back on. Went down the stairs. At the fireplace, before leaving, he turned around.

"My name is Corvus," he said. "If you ever need anything."

The fire flared green and took him.

He stayed on the staircase until dawn. Then he went up to his room, sat on the bed, and didn't unbutton his shirt.

He wore it for three days.

---

"Josh?"

Atis still has her hand on his wrist, on top of the freshly folded sleeve. She's looking at him with her head tilted a little.

"You went away for a second."

"I'm tired." It comes out fine. Almost. "The ritual."

"I know." She pats his cuff, pleased with her work. "That's why I fixed them for you. You looked like a ghost, with your sleeves like that."

He looks at his hands. He sees them. They're there, pale, with a slight tremor the cloth no longer hides, the greenish tint of the veins showing at the wrist. She's looking at them too, without disgust, without fear. The way she looked at the Clabbert. The way one looks at something alive that's worth understanding.

She doesn't know. She doesn't know that a little while ago an elegant man shook her bare hand and asked her surname the way one asks the time. She doesn't know that the same man, eight years ago, stood on a staircase and told a ten-year-old boy there was no need to wait anymore. She doesn't know anything. She fixed his sleeves.

They start down the path.

Halfway down, Josh stops.

He crouches. He searches among the stones on the path. He discards one, another, another, frowning, like someone looking for something very specific and not finding it. He picks one in the end: smooth, grey, just the right size for a palm. He wipes it on his sleeve, without much ceremony, and offers it to her.

"It's for you."

She looks at it. Looks at him.

"There are some Muggle animals," says Josh, choosing each word, "called penguins. They live in very cold places, near the ice. They're birds, but they don't fly. They walk kind of clumsily. In the water they're incredibly fast."

Atis tilts her head. She doesn't know them. It shows.

"When a penguin wants to ask another penguin to be his mate, he looks for the best stone he can find and gives it to her. Just one. The prettiest. If she accepts it, she puts it in her nest, and they stay together." He scratches the back of his neck. Looks down. "They say they choose a mate for life. And that they recognize each other among thousands just by their voice."

A short silence. The wind stirs what's left of the mist among the pines. His hair falls across his forehead and he doesn't push it back.

"I didn't have time to find a better one," he adds, with a half smile, softer than usual. "Next time, I promise."

Atis takes the stone in both hands. It's still warm.

She says nothing. There's no need. She holds it against her chest, and he sees the color rise in her face, and sees her smile with her eyes before her mouth.

Josh starts walking again. She catches up and takes his hand, the one that can be seen now.

*Next time, I promise.* He said it without thinking. He, who promises nothing without thinking. He, who knows exactly how much a given word weighs.

Behind them, the reserve disappears into what's left of the mist. Ahead, the path goes down toward the hedge, toward Hogsmeade, toward the school, toward an ordinary Monday.

*Remember what happened to the last person you couldn't afford to lose.*

He remembers. He remembers with his shirt buttoned to the cuff.

He squeezes Atis's hand a little harder. She squeezes back without asking why.
